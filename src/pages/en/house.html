The House

We found Ie Fushimi before it became Ie.

A main house of 1948 and a rear annex of 1931, joined by a garden. Two storeys of timber under a tile roof. Two old wells. A room below ground, reached by old steps.

It is being read, repaired and lived in again. We did not begin by asking what we could put here. We began by asking what should remain.

Eight chapters of the house

01

Looking upward

Beams cross beams. Above them, the underside of the old roof is open to the room. Some of the timbers are squared; others keep the curve of the tree they were cut from.

Upstairs, a log beam runs the length of the corridor, and a skylight at the far end lets the day in from above. The scale of the house is set overhead. Everything below it — a low table, a cushion, a cup — is measured against it.

02

The garden at the centre

The garden sits between the main house and the rear annex. The rooms on either side face it: through glass, past posts, beyond the edge of the tatami. From a dark room it arrives as a frame of light, with a stone lantern standing in it.

Moss, stone, water and one tree. The garden is a room with the sky as its roof, and it is being tended toward the opening. [planting plan TO CONFIRM]

03

Light enters slowly

Deep eaves and small openings keep most of the house in shade. Light comes in low and sideways. It crosses a lattice and lays bars on a paper screen. It finds the grain of an earthen wall and leaves it again.

Morning finds one surface. Afternoon finds another. We do not want to fill every darkness. Shadow is also part of the room.

04

Water beneath the house

Rain. Ground. Well. Water. Two old wells remain on the site. One will return to the garden. The other, to tea — once the water has been filtered, tested and approved. Drawn. Brewed. Poured.

Fushimi was once also written 伏水.

05

Below the house

An opening in the floor, and old steps going down. The street fades as you descend. At the bottom is one quiet room, below ground.

It will be kept for tea, incense, sound and stillness. We are not going to describe it further here. Some rooms are better found than announced.

06

What the timber remembers

Some of the beams carry writing in a carpenter's ink, still legible. Names, marks, perhaps a date. They are being read and dated now.

Until they are, we will not guess at who wrote them or when. When we know, it will be written here.

07

Small things remain

Three rotary switches on a plastered wall. Wavy glass that bends the garden. A window with timber bars. People lived here. They turned these lights on at night.

These are the things a restoration usually replaces first. Here they stay.

08

Earth and timber

Earthen walls and old wood, both marked by use and by time. Their imperfections are part of the record, and we are leaving them in.

Where something must be added, it will look like what it is. Old and new stay legible, side by side.

Location and arrival

Ie Fushimi sits a few minutes from Fushimi Inari, in one of Kyoto's busiest streets. Outside: crowds, cameras, the shrine. Inside, the house asks for a different pace.
